METHOD CEILING
==============

How high could this pipeline go if every company that has a website were
crawled successfully? Not what it achieved (6 of 500 = 1.2%), but what the
method itself is capped at.

Reproduce with:
  python src/method_ceiling.py

All intervals are Wilson 95%, computed by src/metrics.py:wilson.


1. PUBLICATION RATE
-------------------
Of the domains actually crawled, how many published a valid VAT number?

  6 / 39 = 0.154        Wilson 95% [0.072, 0.297]

Source: data/results/candidates.csv (6 rows) over the 39 strong-match rows in
data/results/sample_domains.csv.


2. SHARE OF THE 500 WITH A FINDABLE WEBSITE
-------------------------------------------
Recomputed from audit/domain_audit.csv, which audited 5 rows from each of the
four outcome groups. The groups are wildly different sizes, so an unweighted
count over the 20 rows would treat the 295-company group and the 39-company
group as equally important. Each group's rate is therefore weighted by the
group's real size.

  group       audited   has a site   rate    Wilson 95%        real size
  strong        5           4        0.800   [0.376, 0.964]        39
  weak          5           3        0.600   [0.231, 0.882]       125
  none          5           2        0.400   [0.118, 0.769]        41
  no_domain     5           3        0.600   [0.231, 0.882]       295

  weighted point estimate: 0.599, about 300 of 500
  weighted range         : [0.233, 0.879]

ASSUMPTION, stated because it drives the number
-----------------------------------------------
"Has a findable website" is read from the auditor's free-text note, not from
the verdict column. The two answer different questions. The verdict answers
"was the pipeline right", so a no_domain row marked `wrong` means the company
does have a site that the pipeline missed, while a strong row marked `wrong`
means the pipeline found a site that belongs to somebody else. Counting
verdicts directly would add those two together and produce nonsense.

Row by row, a company counts as having a findable website when the note shows
a site belonging to that company exists and can be located by a person:

  counted as HAVING a site (12 of 20)
    BSN (LONDON), ETI LOGISTICS SUPPORT, SOUTH YORKSHIRE BUILDERS, TIMGLOBAL
    FISH! RESTAURANTS      fishboroughmarket.com, the generator never tried it
    ADVANCED ROOF TESTING  genuinely theirs
    UNINHIBITED DEV SOLS   uni-concept.netlify.app
    PTERO-BYTE             ptero-byte.com, hyphenated, not the form generated
    DID TEACH              didteach.com, theirs, redirects to thosewhocan.org
    ODYSSEY ELECTRICAL     odyssey-electrical.co.uk exists, unresponsive
    MPL GROUNDWORK         mplcontractors.co.uk exists, suspended
    AJE TECH               ajetech.com exists, crawler got 403

  counted as NOT having one (8 of 20)
    PHSC PLC               phsc.co.uk belongs to a different legal entity
    SURIYA JEWELS          placeholder page, owner not identifiable
    MAPACH                 domain listed for sale
    OUR WORLD OUR SAY      former domain offline
    1COUNSEL, JBB CONSULTANTS, ACE ELECTRICIANS, SBA-SPI   none found

Three of the twelve (ODYSSEY, MPL, AJE TECH) have a site that exists but did
not serve a page: unresponsive, suspended, or 403. They are counted as having
one because the question is what the method could reach at its ceiling, not
what it reached on the day.

That single judgement call moves the answer more than anything else here. All
three sit in the no_domain group, and that group's 5 audited rows stand in for
295 of the 500 companies. Counting them the other way takes no_domain from
3/5 to 0/5 and drags the whole estimate down:

  counted as having a site (used above): share 0.599, ceiling 9.2%
  counted as not having one           : share 0.245, ceiling 3.8%

So the ceiling is somewhere between about 4% and about 9% depending on how
three rows are read. That fragility is the real finding: no estimate resting on
5 rows per group can be quoted to one decimal place, whatever the arithmetic
says.

PHSC is the judgement call in the other direction. It is a listed plc and
phsc.co.uk resolves, but the audit found the site belongs to a subsidiary at
the same premises. A site belonging to another legal entity cannot yield a VAT
number for this one, so it is counted as not having one.


3. CEILING
----------
  ceiling = share with a findable website x publication rate

  point estimate : 0.599 x 0.154 = 0.092, about 9.2%, roughly 46 of 500
  range          : [0.233 x 0.072, 0.879 x 0.297]
                 = [0.017, 0.262], about 1.7% to 26.1%, or 8 to 131 of 500

  achieved       : 6 of 500 = 1.2%

The achieved figure sits at the very bottom of the range, which is what you
would expect from a method losing 461 of 500 at domain discovery.


4. TWO CAVEATS THAT BOTH PUSH THE CEILING UP
--------------------------------------------
a) 6/39 is itself a floor, not the true publication rate. Only 15 candidate
   paths per site were fetched and no PDFs were opened at all. A VAT number on
   a page outside that list, or inside a downloadable invoice, terms document
   or price list, was never looked for. The real share of sites publishing a
   number somewhere is higher than 6/39 by an unknown amount.

b) The 39 strong-match companies are not a random draw from the 500. A company
   whose registered name maps cleanly onto its own domain, and whose site
   states that registered name or company number, is already showing signs of
   caring about formal identity. Companies like that plausibly publish a VAT
   number more often than the other 461. If so, applying 6/39 to the whole
   sample overstates the ceiling rather than understating it, which cuts
   against caveat (a).

Both caveats are unquantified. Neither is a reason to move the number; they are
the reason the range is as wide as it is.


5. WHAT WOULD NARROW THIS
-------------------------
Every interval above rests on 5 audited rows per group. The 50 held-out rows in
data/sample/sample.csv, worked by hand and compared against what the pipeline
produces on the same 50, would replace all four of these 5-row estimates with
one 50-row measurement.
